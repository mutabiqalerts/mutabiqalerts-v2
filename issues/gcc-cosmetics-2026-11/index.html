Cosmetics · GCC · Issue #1

Two GCC cosmetics deadlines land on 31 December

21 November 2026
In short

Saudi Arabia bans cosmetics in syringe-style packaging. Ampoules and vials need four new warnings in Arabic and English. Fix stock by 31 Dec 2026.

Oman now requires 80 fragrance allergens to be declared on labels. Non-compliant imports are banned from 31 Dec 2026.

GSO approved three new cosmetics standards on 17 Sep 2026, and Kuwait has new registration rules with 5-year validity.

🇸🇦  Saudi Arabia · SFDA
Adopted · deadline 31 Dec 2026

Syringe-style cosmetic packaging is banned

SFDA found external-use cosmetics sold in packs that imitate medicines, such as ampoules, vials and syringes. Its Circular No. 6905 (3 August 2026) now bans syringe-format cosmetics outright. Ampoules and vials remain allowed, but both the inner and outer pack must carry four warnings, in Arabic and English, in clear and legible print. No advertising may suggest the product can be injected or used with any tool that penetrates the skin. Companies have until 31 December 2026 to recall affected products and correct their status.

Who it affects: Serum, booster and "skin-booster" brands, and their Saudi importers.

Sources: [1] [2]
🇴🇲  Oman · Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion
Adopted · imports from 31 Dec 2026

80 fragrance allergens must now appear on the label

A ministerial circular (June 2026) adds 80 allergens to Annex 3 of the GCC cosmetics technical regulation (GSO 1943/2024), with the EU Cosmetics Regulation as a supplementary reference. These must be declared in the label's ingredient list. From 31 December 2026, importing or manufacturing non-compliant products is prohibited. Products already on shelves have until 31 December 2028.

Who it affects: Anyone selling perfumed cosmetics or personal care products in Oman: creams, shampoos, deodorants and fragrances.

Sources: [3] [4]
🌐  GCC-wide · GSO
Approved 17 Sep 2026

Three new Gulf standards for cosmetics

GSO 2815:2026 sets out how to label fragrance allergens, so it's the practical companion to Oman's circular. GSO 2816:2026 sets GCC-wide requirements for making, evaluating, notifying and selling cosmetics that contain nanomaterials. GSO ISO 23698:2026 adopts a lab method that measures SPF and UVA protection without human volunteer testing. Gulf standards become mandatory only when a country's regulation references them, so ask your importer how your target market applies them.

Who it affects: Brands with perfumed products, nano-form ingredients (e.g. nano zinc oxide or titanium dioxide) or SPF claims.

Sources: [5] [6] [7]
🇰🇼  Kuwait · Ministry of Health
In force since Dec 2025

New registration rules, valid for five years

Ministerial Resolution No. 344 of 2025 resets how cosmetics are registered. You register through a licensed local agent, with a legalised agency certificate and a GMP or ISO 22716 certificate for the factory. Registration is valid for five years, and you must renew six months before it expires. Rejections can be appealed within 90 days.

Who it affects: Foreign brands selling in Kuwait, and their agents.

Sources: [8]

✅ Your checklist before 31 December

1. Saudi Arabia: list every SKU sold in a syringe, ampoule or vial. Syringes must be repackaged. Ampoules and vials need the 4 bilingual warnings on the inner and outer pack.
2. Saudi Arabia: remove "injectable", "needle-free filler" and similar wording from product pages, ads and influencer briefs.
3. Oman: ask your fragrance supplier for the allergen breakdown of each perfume you use. They usually provide it free, and it's all you need to update the ingredient list.
4. Oman: relabel anything shipping after 31 Dec 2026. Plan stock already on shelves for relabelling by 31 Dec 2028.
5. Kuwait: check that your agent holds a Ministry of Health import licence and warehouse licence, and put your renewal date in the calendar six months early.
💡 Do it yourself before paying anyoneMost of this is label work, not lab work. You can do the SKU list, the allergen breakdown from your perfume supplier and the label redesign in-house. Pay a consultant or an SFDA-approved conformity body only for the parts you can't do yourself. A rejected shipment costs far more than a label reprint.
